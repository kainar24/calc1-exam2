1. Relative extrema

Definition
  • $f$ has a relative maximum at $x_0$ if $f(x_0)\ge f(x)$ for all $x$ in some open interval containing $x_0$.
  • $f$ has a relative minimum at $x_0$ if $f(x_0)\le f(x)$ for all $x$ in some open interval containing $x_0$.
  • A relative extremum is either of them.
Picture from the slide: a mountain range. Every peak is a relative maximum; the highest peak is the absolute maximum. Every valley is a relative minimum; the deepest valley is the absolute minimum.

Lecture examples (from graphs): (a) $y=x^2$: relative min at $x=0$, no relative max. (b) $y=x^3$: no relative extrema. (c) $y=x^3-3x+3$: relative max at $x=-1$, relative min at $x=1$. (d) $y=\frac12x^4-\frac43x^3-x^2+4x+1$: relative minima at $x=-1$ and $x=2$, relative max at $x=1$. (e) $y=\cos x$: relative maxima at all even multiples of $\pi$, relative minima at all odd multiples of $\pi$.

2. Critical and stationary points

Remarks. Relative extrema occur at points with horizontal tangents, but they can also occur at points of non-differentiability (corners, cusps).

Definition
(a) Critical points: points in the domain of $f$ at which either $f$ has a horizontal tangent ($f'=0$) or $f$ is not differentiable.
(b) Stationary points: critical points at which $f'(x)=0$ (horizontal tangent).
So every stationary point is critical, but not every critical point is stationary.
Theorem
Suppose $f$ is defined on an open interval containing $x_0$. If $f$ has a relative extremum at $x_0$, then $x_0$ is a critical point of $f$: either $f'(x_0)=0$ or $f$ is not differentiable at $x_0$.
The converse is false: a critical point need not be an extremum ($x^3$ at $0$).
Lecture example: critical and stationary points of $f(x)=3x^{5/3}-15x^{2/3}$
  1. $f'(x)=5x^{2/3}-10x^{-1/3}=5x^{-1/3}(x-2)=\dfrac{5(x-2)}{x^{1/3}}$.
  2. $f'(x)=0\iff x=2$; $f'$ is undefined at $x=0$ (and $0$ is in the domain).
  3. Critical points: $x=0,2$. Stationary point: $x=2$ only.

3. First derivative test

Theorem (First Derivative Test)
A function has a relative extremum at those critical points where $f'$ changes sign. Suppose $f$ is continuous at a critical point $x_0$.
  1. If $f'>0$ on an open interval extending left from $x_0$ and $f'<0$ on an open interval extending right, then $f$ has a relative maximum at $x_0$.
  2. If $f'<0$ on the left and $f'>0$ on the right, then $f$ has a relative minimum at $x_0$.
  3. If $f'$ has the same sign on both sides, then $f$ has no relative extremum at $x_0$.

The slides show 8 pictures: a smooth peak (critical, stationary, max), a sharp peak (critical, not stationary, max), a smooth valley (critical, stationary, min), a V-corner (critical, not stationary, min), and four pictures where the curve keeps rising or keeps falling through $x_0$, with a horizontal tangent or a vertical one. Those are critical points that are inflection points, not extrema.

Lecture example: relative extrema of $f(x)=3x^{5/3}-15x^{2/3}$
  1. $f'(x)=\dfrac{5(x-2)}{x^{1/3}}$, critical points $x=0,2$.
  2. Sign table: $x<0$: $\frac{(-)}{(-)}=+$; $0<x<2$: $\frac{(-)}{(+)}=-$; $x>2$: $\frac{(+)}{(+)}=+$.
  3. $+\to-$ at $x=0$: relative maximum at $x=0$ (a cusp, $f(0)=0$). $-\to+$ at $x=2$: relative minimum at $x=2$, $f(2)=3\cdot2^{5/3}-15\cdot2^{2/3}=-9\sqrt[3]4\approx-14.3$.

4. Second derivative test

Theorem (Second Derivative Test)
Suppose $f$ is twice differentiable at $x_0$.
  1. If $f'(x_0)=0$ and $f''(x_0)<0$, then $f$ has a relative maximum at $x_0$ (concave down, like a cap).
  2. If $f'(x_0)=0$ and $f''(x_0)>0$, then $f$ has a relative minimum at $x_0$ (concave up, like a cup).
  3. If $f'(x_0)=0$ and $f''(x_0)=0$, the test is inconclusive: $f$ may have a relative max, a relative min, or neither. Use the first derivative test.
It only works at stationary points (not at points where $f'$ does not exist).
Lecture example: $f(x)=3x^5-5x^3$
  1. $f'(x)=15x^4-15x^2=15x^2(x+1)(x-1)$; stationary points $x=0,-1,1$.
  2. $f''(x)=60x^3-30x=30x(2x^2-1)$.
  3. $x=-1$: $f''=-30<0$ ⇒ relative max. $x=1$: $f''=30>0$ ⇒ relative min. $x=0$: $f''=0$ ⇒ inconclusive.
  4. First derivative test at $x=0$: on $(-1,0)$ and on $(0,1)$ the signs are $(+)(+)(-)$, so $f'<0$ on both sides. No sign change ⇒ no relative extremum at $x=0$.
Lecture example: $f(x)=e^{-x^2/2}$

$f'(x)=-xe^{-x^2/2}$, critical point $x=0$; $f''(x)=(x^2-1)e^{-x^2/2}$, $f''(0)=-1<0$ ⇒ relative maximum at $x=0$.

Lecture example: $f(x)=\sin2x$, $0<x<\pi$
  1. $f'(x)=2\cos2x=0\Rightarrow 2x=\frac\pi2,\frac{3\pi}2\Rightarrow x=\frac\pi4,\frac{3\pi}4$.
  2. $f''(x)=-4\sin2x$: $f''(\frac\pi4)=-4<0$ (relative max), $f''(\frac{3\pi}4)=4>0$ (relative min).

5. Graphing polynomials

Multiplicity of a root
A root $x=r$ of a polynomial $p$ has multiplicity $m$ if $(x-r)^m$ divides $p(x)$ but $(x-r)^{m+1}$ does not. A root of multiplicity 1 is a simple root.
Example: $p(x)=(x-2)(x-1)^5$ has degree 6, so six roots: $x=1$ repeated 5 times and $x=2$ (simple).
Theorem (geometric meaning of multiplicity)
Let $p$ have a root of multiplicity $m$ at $x=r$.
  1. $m$ even: the graph is tangent to the $x$-axis at $r$, does not cross it, and has no inflection point there.
  2. $m$ odd, $m>1$: the graph is tangent to the $x$-axis, crosses it, and has an inflection point there.
  3. $m=1$: the graph is not tangent to the axis, crosses it, and may or may not have an inflection point there.
Lecture example: $p(x)=x^3(3x-4)(x+2)^2$

$x$-intercepts $0,\frac43,-2$. $x=0$ has multiplicity 3 (odd $>1$): tangent, crosses, inflection point. $x=-2$ has multiplicity 2 (even): tangent, does not cross, no inflection. $x=\frac43$ is simple: crosses without being tangent.

Properties of polynomials used in sketching. Natural domain $(-\infty,\infty)$; continuous and differentiable everywhere (no corners, no vertical tangents); $p(x)\to\pm\infty$ as $x\to\pm\infty$; a polynomial of degree $n>1$ has at most $n$ $x$-intercepts, $n-1$ relative extrema and $n-2$ inflection points.

Lecture example: sketch $y=x^3-3x+2$
  1. $x$-intercepts: $x^3-3x+2=(x+2)(x-1)^2$, so $x=-2$ and $x=1$ (double root: the graph touches the axis there). $y$-intercept: $y=2$.
  2. End behaviour: $y\to+\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$, $y\to-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$.
  3. $y'=3x^2-3=3(x-1)(x+1)$: $+$ on $(-\infty,-1)$, $-$ on $(-1,1)$, $+$ on $(1,\infty)$. Relative max $(-1,4)$, relative min $(1,0)$.
  4. $y''=6x$: concave down on $(-\infty,0)$, concave up on $(0,\infty)$, inflection point $(0,2)$.
  5. Plot the points $(-2,0)$, $(-1,4)$, $(0,2)$, $(1,0)$ and join them following the sign lines.

Summary: how to sketch a polynomial (the lecture's recipe)

  1. Intercepts (factor; note the multiplicities).
  2. End behaviour (leading term).
  3. $f'$ sign line ⇒ increase/decrease and relative extrema.
  4. $f''$ sign line ⇒ concavity and inflection points.
  5. Plot the key points and draw.
