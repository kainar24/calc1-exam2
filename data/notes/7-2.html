1. Deriving the formula (product rule backwards)

Derivation
Consider $\int f(x)g(x)\,dx$ and let $G$ be an antiderivative of $g$ ($G'=g$).
  • Product rule: $\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)G(x)]=f(x)G'(x)+f'(x)G(x)=f(x)g(x)+f'(x)G(x)$.
  • Integrate: $\int[f(x)g(x)+f'(x)G(x)]\,dx=f(x)G(x)$, i.e. $\displaystyle\int f(x)g(x)\,dx=f(x)G(x)-\int f'(x)G(x)\,dx$.
  • Put $u=f(x)$, $v=G(x)$, so $du=f'(x)\,dx$, $dv=g(x)\,dx$:
$$\boxed{\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du}\qquad\text{(Integration by Parts)}$$
Strategy (from the slide)
Choose $dv$ so that the new integral $\int v\,du$ is easier than the original, and choose $u$ so that it becomes simpler when differentiated (LIATE: Log, Inverse trig, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential).

2. Basic examples

$\int x\cos x\,dx$

$u=x$, $dv=\cos x\,dx$ ⇒ $du=dx$, $v=\sin x$: $\int x\cos x\,dx=x\sin x-\int\sin x\,dx=x\sin x+\cos x+C$.

$\int xe^x\,dx$

$u=x$, $dv=e^xdx$ ⇒ $du=dx$, $v=e^x$: $xe^x-\int e^xdx=xe^x-e^x+C=e^x(x-1)+C$. Check: $\frac{d}{dx}[e^x(x-1)]=e^x(x-1)+e^x=xe^x$ ✓.

$\int\ln x\,dx$ (take $dv=dx$)

$u=\ln x$, $dv=dx$ ⇒ $du=\frac1xdx$, $v=x$: $x\ln x-\int x\cdot\frac1x\,dx=x\ln x-x+C$. Check: $\frac{d}{dx}(x\ln x-x)=\ln x+1-1=\ln x$ ✓.

3. Repeated integration by parts

$\int x^2e^{-x}\,dx$ (twice)
  1. First: $u=x^2$, $dv=e^{-x}dx$ ⇒ $du=2x\,dx$, $v=-e^{-x}$: $\int x^2e^{-x}dx=-x^2e^{-x}+2\int xe^{-x}dx$.
  2. Second: $u=x$, $dv=e^{-x}dx$ ⇒ $\int xe^{-x}dx=-xe^{-x}+\int e^{-x}dx=-xe^{-x}-e^{-x}+C$.
  3. Combine: $-x^2e^{-x}+2(-xe^{-x}-e^{-x})+C=-e^{-x}(x^2+2x+2)+C$.
$\int e^x\cos x\,dx$ (twice, then solve for $I$)
  1. Call it $I$. First: $u=\cos x$, $dv=e^xdx$ ⇒ $du=-\sin x\,dx$, $v=e^x$: $I=e^x\cos x+\int e^x\sin x\,dx$.
  2. Second: $u=\sin x$, $dv=e^xdx$: $\int e^x\sin x\,dx=e^x\sin x-\int e^x\cos x\,dx=e^x\sin x-I$.
  3. So $I=e^x\cos x+e^x\sin x-I\Rightarrow2I=e^x(\sin x+\cos x)$.
  4. $\displaystyle\int e^x\cos x\,dx=\frac{e^x(\sin x+\cos x)}2+C$.

4. Tabular integration by parts

For $\int p(x)f(x)\,dx$, $p$ a polynomial
  1. Differentiate $p(x)$ repeatedly until you get $0$; list the results in the first column.
  2. Integrate $f(x)$ repeatedly; list the results in the second column.
  3. Draw an arrow from each entry of the first column to the entry one row down in the second column.
  4. Label the arrows with alternating signs $+,-,+,\dots$, starting with $+$.
  5. Multiply tip × tail for each arrow with its sign, and add.
$\int(x^2-x)\cos x\,dx$
D$x^2-x$$2x-1$$2$$0$
I$\cos x$$\sin x$$-\cos x$$-\sin x$

$(x^2-x)\sin x+(2x-1)\cos x-2\sin x+C=(x^2-x-2)\sin x+(2x-1)\cos x+C$.

$\int x^2\sqrt{x-1}\,dx$
D$x^2$$2x$$2$$0$
I$(x-1)^{1/2}$$\frac23(x-1)^{3/2}$$\frac4{15}(x-1)^{5/2}$$\frac8{105}(x-1)^{7/2}$

$\frac23x^2(x-1)^{3/2}-\frac8{15}x(x-1)^{5/2}+\frac{16}{105}(x-1)^{7/2}+C$. (Same integral as in §5.3, done by substitution there. Both answers are correct.)

5. Definite integrals by parts

Formula
$$\int_a^bu\,dv=uv\Big|_a^b-\int_a^bv\,du.$$
$\int_0^1\tan^{-1}x\,dx$
  1. $u=\tan^{-1}x$, $dv=dx$ ⇒ $du=\frac{dx}{1+x^2}$, $v=x$.
  2. $\int_0^1\tan^{-1}x\,dx=x\tan^{-1}x\Big|_0^1-\int_0^1\frac{x}{1+x^2}dx$.
  3. First term: $1\cdot\frac\pi4-0=\frac\pi4$. Second, with $w=1+x^2$: $\int_0^1\frac{x\,dx}{1+x^2}=\frac12\ln(1+x^2)\Big|_0^1=\frac12\ln2$.
  4. Result: $\frac\pi4-\frac12\ln2$.
