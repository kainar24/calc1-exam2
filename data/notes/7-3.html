1. Reduction formulas for $\sin^n$ and $\cos^n$

Definition
Reduction formulas express an integral of a power of a function through an integral of a lower power of that function. For $n\ge2$: $$\int\sin^nx\,dx=-\frac1n\sin^{n-1}x\cos x+\frac{n-1}n\int\sin^{n-2}x\,dx,$$ $$\int\cos^nx\,dx=\frac1n\cos^{n-1}x\sin x+\frac{n-1}n\int\cos^{n-2}x\,dx.$$
Proof of the first formula (integration by parts)
  1. Let $I_n=\int\sin^nx\,dx$. Write $\sin^nx=\sin^{n-1}x\sin x$: $u=\sin^{n-1}x$, $dv=\sin x\,dx$ ⇒ $du=(n-1)\sin^{n-2}x\cos x\,dx$, $v=-\cos x$.
  2. $I_n=-\sin^{n-1}x\cos x+(n-1)\int\sin^{n-2}x\cos^2x\,dx$.
  3. $\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x$: $\int\sin^{n-2}x\cos^2x\,dx=I_{n-2}-I_n$.
  4. $I_n=-\sin^{n-1}x\cos x+(n-1)I_{n-2}-(n-1)I_n\Rightarrow nI_n=-\sin^{n-1}x\cos x+(n-1)I_{n-2}$. Divide by $n$. ∎
Lecture examples: $\int\sin^2x\,dx$, $\int\sin^4x\,dx$, $\int\sin^3x\,dx$, $\int\cos^3x\,dx$
  • $n=2$: $\int\sin^2x\,dx=-\frac12\sin x\cos x+\frac12\int1\,dx=-\frac12\sin x\cos x+\frac x2+C$.
  • $n=4$: $\int\sin^4x\,dx=-\frac14\sin^3x\cos x+\frac34\int\sin^2x\,dx=-\frac14\sin^3x\cos x-\frac38\sin x\cos x+\frac{3x}8+C$.
  • $n=3$: $\int\sin^3x\,dx=-\frac13\sin^2x\cos x+\frac23\int\sin x\,dx=-\frac13\sin^2x\cos x-\frac23\cos x+C$.
  • $\int\cos^3x\,dx=\frac13\cos^2x\sin x+\frac23\int\cos x\,dx=\frac13\cos^2x\sin x+\frac23\sin x+C$.
Squares: the half-angle way (faster)
$\sin^2x=\frac12(1-\cos2x)$, $\cos^2x=\frac12(1+\cos2x)$, so $$\int\sin^2x\,dx=\frac x2-\frac14\sin2x+C,\qquad\int\cos^2x\,dx=\frac x2+\frac14\sin2x+C.$$ (The same as the reduction-formula answers, since $\sin2x=2\sin x\cos x$.)

2. Products $\int\sin^mx\cos^nx\,dx$

caseprocedureidentity
$n$ oddsplit off one $\cos x$, convert the rest, $u=\sin x$$\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x$
$m$ oddsplit off one $\sin x$, convert the rest, $u=\cos x$$\sin^2x=1-\cos^2x$
$m,n$ both evenreduce the powers with half-angle formulas$\sin^2x=\frac12(1-\cos2x)$, $\cos^2x=\frac12(1+\cos2x)$
Lecture example: $\int\sin^4x\cos^5x\,dx$ ($n$ odd)
  1. $=\int\sin^4x\cos^4x\cos x\,dx=\int\sin^4x(1-\sin^2x)^2\cos x\,dx$.
  2. $u=\sin x$: $\int u^4(1-u^2)^2du=\int(u^4-2u^6+u^8)\,du=\frac15u^5-\frac27u^7+\frac19u^9+C$.
  3. $=\frac15\sin^5x-\frac27\sin^7x+\frac19\sin^9x+C$.
Lecture example: $\int\sin^4x\cos^4x\,dx$ (both even)
  1. $=\int\left(\frac12[1-\cos2x]\right)^2\left(\frac12[1+\cos2x]\right)^2dx=\frac1{16}\int(1-\cos^22x)^2dx=\frac1{16}\int\sin^42x\,dx$.
  2. $u=2x$, $dx=\frac12du$: $\frac1{32}\int\sin^4u\,du=\frac1{32}\left(\frac38u-\frac14\sin2u+\frac1{32}\sin4u\right)+C$.
  3. $=\frac3{128}x-\frac1{128}\sin4x+\frac1{1024}\sin8x+C$.

Product-to-sum identities
$\sin\alpha\cos\beta=\frac12[\sin(\alpha-\beta)+\sin(\alpha+\beta)]$
$\sin\alpha\sin\beta=\frac12[\cos(\alpha-\beta)-\cos(\alpha+\beta)]$
$\cos\alpha\cos\beta=\frac12[\cos(\alpha-\beta)+\cos(\alpha+\beta)]$

4. Tangent and secant

Basic formulas
$$\int\tan x\,dx=\ln|\sec x|+C,\qquad\int\sec x\,dx=\ln|\sec x+\tan x|+C.$$ Proof of the first: $\int\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}dx$, $u=\cos x$, $du=-\sin x\,dx$: $-\int\frac{du}u=-\ln|\cos x|+C=\ln|\sec x|+C$.
Reduction formulas
$$\int\tan^nx\,dx=\frac{\tan^{n-1}x}{n-1}-\int\tan^{n-2}x\,dx,\qquad\int\sec^nx\,dx=\frac{\sec^{n-2}x\tan x}{n-1}+\frac{n-2}{n-1}\int\sec^{n-2}x\,dx.$$ Examples: $\int\tan^2x\,dx=\tan x-x+C$; $\int\sec^2x\,dx=\tan x+C$; $\int\tan^3x\,dx=\frac12\tan^2x-\ln|\sec x|+C$; $\int\sec^3x\,dx=\frac12\sec x\tan x+\frac12\ln|\sec x+\tan x|+C$.

5. Products $\int\tan^mx\sec^nx\,dx$

caseprocedureidentity
$n$ evensplit off $\sec^2x$, convert the rest, $u=\tan x$$\sec^2x=\tan^2x+1$
$m$ oddsplit off $\sec x\tan x$, convert the rest, $u=\sec x$$\tan^2x=\sec^2x-1$
$m$ even, $n$ oddreduce to powers of $\sec x$ alone, then use the reduction formula for $\sec^n$$\tan^2x=\sec^2x-1$
Lecture example: $\int\tan^3x\sec^3x\,dx$ ($m$ odd)
  1. $=\int\tan^2x\sec^2x(\sec x\tan x)\,dx=\int(\sec^2x-1)\sec^2x(\sec x\tan x)\,dx$.
  2. $u=\sec x$, $du=\sec x\tan x\,dx$: $\int(u^2-1)u^2du=\frac15u^5-\frac13u^3+C=\frac15\sec^5x-\frac13\sec^3x+C$.
Lecture example: $\int\tan^2x\sec^4x\,dx$ ($n$ even)
  1. $=\int\tan^2x\sec^2x\sec^2x\,dx=\int\tan^2x(\tan^2x+1)\sec^2x\,dx$.
  2. $u=\tan x$, $du=\sec^2x\,dx$: $\int u^2(u^2+1)\,du=\frac15u^5+\frac13u^3+C=\frac15\tan^5x+\frac13\tan^3x+C$.
