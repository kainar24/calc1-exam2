1. Why substitution works

Procedure (method of $u$-substitution)
  • Chain rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}[F(g(x))]=F'(g(x))\,g'(x)$.
  • So $\displaystyle\int F'(g(x))g'(x)\,dx=\int\frac{d}{dx}[F(g(x))]\,dx=F(g(x))+C$.
  • If $F'=f$: $\displaystyle\int f(g(x))\,g'(x)\,dx=F(g(x))+C$.
  • Let $u=g(x)$. Then $\frac{du}{dx}=g'(x)$, i.e. $du=g'(x)\,dx$, and the integral becomes $\displaystyle\int f(u)\,du=F(u)+C$.
Steps: choose $u$ (an “inside” function whose derivative also appears), compute $du$, rewrite everything in terms of $u$, integrate, substitute back.

2. Lecture examples

$\int(x^2+1)^{50}\cdot2x\,dx$

$u=x^2+1$, $du=2x\,dx$: $\int u^{50}du=\frac{u^{51}}{51}+C=\frac{(x^2+1)^{51}}{51}+C$.

$\int\frac{dx}{1+3x^2}$

$u=\sqrt3x$, $du=\sqrt3\,dx$: $\frac1{\sqrt3}\int\frac{du}{1+u^2}=\frac1{\sqrt3}\tan^{-1}u+C=\frac1{\sqrt3}\tan^{-1}(\sqrt3x)+C$.

$\int\sin(x+9)\,dx$

$u=x+9$, $du=dx$: $\int\sin u\,du=-\cos u+C=-\cos(x+9)+C$.

$\int\frac{dx}{\left(\frac13x-8\right)^5}$

$u=\frac13x-8$, $du=\frac13dx$ so $dx=3\,du$: $\int\frac{3\,du}{u^5}=3\int u^{-5}du=-\frac34u^{-4}+C=-\frac34\left(\frac13x-8\right)^{-4}+C$.

$\int\left(\frac1x+\sec^2\pi x\right)dx$

$=\ln|x|+\int\sec^2\pi x\,dx$; with $u=\pi x$, $dx=\frac1\pi du$: $\frac1\pi\int\sec^2u\,du=\frac1\pi\tan u$. Answer: $\ln|x|+\frac1\pi\tan\pi x+C$.

$\int t^4\sqrt[3]{3-5t^5}\,dt$

$u=3-5t^5$, $du=-25t^4dt$, so $t^4dt=-\frac1{25}du$: $-\frac1{25}\int u^{1/3}du=-\frac1{25}\cdot\frac{u^{4/3}}{4/3}+C=-\frac3{100}(3-5t^5)^{4/3}+C$.

$\int x^2\sqrt{x-1}\,dx$ (leftover $x$ expressed in $u$)
  1. $u=x-1$, $du=dx$, $x=u+1$ so $x^2=u^2+2u+1$.
  2. $\int(u^2+2u+1)\sqrt u\,du=\int\left(u^{5/2}+2u^{3/2}+u^{1/2}\right)du=\frac27u^{7/2}+\frac45u^{5/2}+\frac23u^{3/2}+C$.
  3. $=\frac27(x-1)^{7/2}+\frac45(x-1)^{5/2}+\frac23(x-1)^{3/2}+C$.
$\int\cos^3x\,dx$ (a preview of §7.3)

$\cos^3x=(1-\sin^2x)\cos x$; $u=\sin x$, $du=\cos x\,dx$: $\int(1-u^2)\,du=u-\frac{u^3}3+C=\sin x-\frac13\sin^3x+C$.

3. Special formulas (memorize)

For $a>0$
$$\int\frac{du}{a^2+u^2}=\frac1a\tan^{-1}\frac ua+C,\qquad\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{a^2-u^2}}=\sin^{-1}\frac ua+C,\qquad\int\frac{du}{u\sqrt{u^2-a^2}}=\frac1a\sec^{-1}\left|\frac ua\right|+C.$$
Lecture example: $\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{2-x^2}}$

$u=x$, $a=\sqrt2$: $\sin^{-1}\frac x{\sqrt2}+C$.

Typical exam versions: $\int\frac{4x^3}{9+x^8}dx$ ($u=x^4$, $a=3$), $\int\frac{x^3}{\sqrt{1-x^8}}dx$ ($u=x^4$, $a=1$). See Practice for full solutions.
