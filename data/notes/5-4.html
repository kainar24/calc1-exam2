1. Sigma notation

Definition
To write a long sum compactly we use sigma ($\Sigma$) notation: $1^2+2^2+3^2+4^2+5^2=\sum_{k=1}^5k^2$, “the summation of $k^2$, where $k$ runs from 1 to 5”. In general, for integers $m\le n$, $\sum_{k=m}^nf(k)$: $m$ is the starting value of $k$, $n$ the ending value.

Lecture examples: $\sum_{k=0}^5(2k+1)=1+3+5+7+9+11$; $\ \sum_{k=0}^5(-1)^k(2k+1)=1-3+5-7+9-11$; $\ \sum_{k=-3}^1k^3=-27-8-1+0+1$; $\ \sum_{k=1}^3k\sin\frac{k\pi}5=\sin\frac\pi5+2\sin\frac{2\pi}5+3\sin\frac{3\pi}5$.

  • The name of the index does not matter: $\sum_{i=1}^6\frac1i=\sum_{j=1}^6\frac1j=\sum_{k=1}^6\frac1k$.
  • Equal limits give a single term: $\sum_{i=2}^2\frac1i=\frac12$, $\ \sum_{k=n}^n\frac1{k+2}=\frac1{n+2}$.
  • A summand that does not depend on the index is just repeated: $\sum_{i=1}^52=2(5)$, $\ \sum_{k=0}^2x^3=3x^3$ (note: $k=0,1,2$ is three terms).
  • Dummy indices can be shifted: $\sum_{i=1}^52i=\sum_{j=0}^4(2j+2)=\sum_{k=3}^7(2k-4)$.
Theorem (properties)
$\sum_{k=1}^nca_k=c\sum_{k=1}^na_k$ ($c$ independent of $k$), $\qquad\sum(a_k\pm b_k)=\sum a_k\pm\sum b_k$.
Theorem (closed forms)
$$\sum_{k=1}^nk=\frac{n(n+1)}2,\qquad\sum_{k=1}^nk^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6,\qquad\sum_{k=1}^nk^3=\left[\frac{n(n+1)}2\right]^2,\qquad\sum_{k=1}^n1=n.$$
Lecture example: $\sum_{k=1}^{30}k(k+1)$

$=\sum k^2+\sum k=\frac{30\cdot31\cdot61}6+\frac{30\cdot31}2=9455+465=9920$.

Lecture example: closed form of $\sum_{k=1}^n(3+k)^2$
  1. $\sum_{k=1}^n(3+k)^2=4^2+5^2+\dots+(n+3)^2=\left[1^2+2^2+\dots+(n+3)^2\right]-\left[1^2+2^2+3^2\right]$.
  2. $=\sum_{k=1}^{n+3}k^2-14=\frac{(n+3)(n+4)(2n+7)}6-14=\frac16\left(2n^3+21n^2+73n\right)$.

2. Area as a limit

Problem: $f$ continuous and nonnegative on $[a,b]$; find the area $A$ of the region $R$ under $y=f(x)$.

  1. Divide $[a,b]$ into $n$ equal subintervals of width $\Delta x=\frac{b-a}n$ with points $a=x_0<x_1<\dots<x_n=b$.
  2. In each subinterval $[x_{k-1},x_k]$ choose a point $x_k^*$ and build a rectangle of height $f(x_k^*)$.
  3. $A\approx\sum_{k=1}^nf(x_k^*)\Delta x$. This sum is a Riemann sum.
Definition (area under a curve)
If $f$ is continuous and nonnegative on $[a,b]$, the area under $y=f(x)$ over $[a,b]$ is $$A=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^nf(x_k^*)\Delta x.$$ The points $x_k^*$ can be chosen arbitrarily in $[x_{k-1},x_k]$ (the limit is the same). Typical choices:
  • left endpoints: $x_k^*=x_{k-1}=a+(k-1)\Delta x$;
  • right endpoints: $x_k^*=x_k=a+k\Delta x$;
  • midpoints: $x_k^*=\frac12(x_{k-1}+x_k)=a+\left(k-\frac12\right)\Delta x$.
Lecture example: area under $f(x)=x^2$ over $[0,1]$, right endpoints
  1. $\Delta x=\frac{1-0}n=\frac1n$, $x_k^*=0+k\Delta x=\frac kn$.
  2. $\sum_{k=1}^n(x_k^*)^2\Delta x=\sum\left(\frac kn\right)^2\frac1n=\frac1{n^3}\sum_{k=1}^nk^2=\frac1{n^3}\cdot\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6$.
  3. $=\frac16\left(\frac nn\cdot\frac{n+1}n\cdot\frac{2n+1}n\right)=\frac16\left(1+\frac1n\right)\left(2+\frac1n\right)$.
  4. $A=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac16\left(1+\frac1n\right)\left(2+\frac1n\right)=\frac16\cdot1\cdot2=\frac13$.
Lecture example: approximate the area under $y=9-x^2$ on $[0,3]$ with $n=10,20,50$

$\Delta x=\frac3n$. For $n=10$ ($\Delta x=0.3$): left endpoints give $0.3\cdot64.35=19.305$, right endpoints $0.3\cdot55.35=16.605$, midpoints $0.3\cdot60.075=18.0225$.

3. Net signed area

Definition (net signed area)
If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ (any sign), the net signed area between $y=f(x)$ and $[a,b]$ is $A=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^nf(x_k^*)\Delta x$. Rectangles below the axis contribute negatively: $A=A_{\rm I}+A_{\rm III}-A_{\rm II}=$ (area above) $-$ (area below).
Lecture example: net signed area for $f(x)=x-1$ on $[0,2]$, left endpoints
  1. $\Delta x=\frac2n$, $x_k^*=x_{k-1}=(k-1)\frac2n$.
  2. $\sum(x_k^*-1)\Delta x=\sum\left[(k-1)\frac2n-1\right]\frac2n=\sum\left[\frac4{n^2}k-\frac4{n^2}-\frac2n\right]$.
  3. $=\frac4{n^2}\cdot\frac{n(n+1)}2-\frac4{n^2}\cdot n-\frac2n\cdot n=\frac{2(n+1)}n-\frac4n-2=-\frac2n$.
  4. $A=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(-\frac2n\right)=0$. Geometrically, the triangle below the axis ($A_2=\frac12$) cancels the triangle above ($A_1=\frac12$).

Exam recipe (right endpoints)

  1. $\Delta x=\frac{b-a}n$, $x_k=a+k\Delta x$.
  2. Write $f(x_k)\Delta x$ and expand it into powers of $k$.
  3. Pull out constants; use the closed forms for $\sum1,\sum k,\sum k^2,\sum k^3$.
  4. Simplify to an expression in $n$, then let $n\to\infty$.
  5. Check with the FTC.
