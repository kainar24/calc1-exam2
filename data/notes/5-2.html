1. Antiderivatives

Definition (antiderivative)
$F$ is an antiderivative of $f$ on an open interval if $F'(x)=f(x)$ for all $x$ in the interval.
Example: $F(x)=\frac13x^3$ is an antiderivative of $f(x)=x^2$ on $(-\infty,\infty)$, because $\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac13x^3\right]=x^2$.
Theorem
If $F$ is an antiderivative of $f$ on an open interval, then $F(x)+C$ is also an antiderivative for any constant $C$, and every antiderivative of $f$ on the interval has the form $F(x)+C$. (The second part follows from the MVT: two functions with the same derivative differ by a constant.)

2. The indefinite integral

Definitions
The process of finding antiderivatives is called antidifferentiation or integration. If $F'(x)=f(x)$, we write $$\int f(x)\,dx=F(x)+C,$$ where $C$ is an arbitrary constant. $\int f(x)\,dx$ is the indefinite integral; $\int$ (an “elongated S”) is the integral sign; $f(x)$ is the integrand; $dx$ identifies the independent variable.
Differentiating an antiderivative gives the integrand back: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[\int f(x)\,dx\right]=f(x)$.

Every derivative formula gives an integration formula: $\frac{d}{dx}[x^3]=3x^2\iff\int3x^2dx=x^3+C$; $\frac{d}{dx}[\sqrt x]=\frac1{2\sqrt x}\iff\int\frac{dx}{2\sqrt x}=\sqrt x+C$; $\frac{d}{dt}[\tan t]=\sec^2t\iff\int\sec^2t\,dt=\tan t+C$.

Lecture examples: the power rule

$\int x^2dx=\frac{x^3}3+C$ ($r=2$); $\ \int x^3dx=\frac{x^4}4+C$ ($r=3$); $\ \int\frac{dx}{x^5}=\int x^{-5}dx=\frac{x^{-4}}{-4}+C=-\frac1{4x^4}+C$ ($r=-5$); $\ \int\sqrt x\,dx=\int x^{1/2}dx=\frac{x^{3/2}}{3/2}+C=\frac23(\sqrt x)^3+C$ ($r=\frac12$).

Theorem (properties of the indefinite integral)
If $f,g$ have antiderivatives and $c$ is a constant: $$\int cf(x)\,dx=c\int f(x)\,dx,\qquad\int[f(x)\pm g(x)]\,dx=\int f(x)\,dx\pm\int g(x)\,dx.$$ (There is no product or quotient rule for integrals: simplify first.)
Lecture example: $\int(3x^6-2x^2+7x+1)\,dx$

$=3\int x^6dx-2\int x^2dx+7\int x\,dx+\int1\,dx=\frac{3x^7}7-\frac{2x^3}3+\frac{7x^2}2+x+C$.

Lecture example: $\int\frac{t^2-2t^4}{t^4}\,dt$ (divide first)

$=\int\left(\frac1{t^2}-2\right)dt=\int(t^{-2}-2)\,dt=\frac{t^{-1}}{-1}-2t+C=-\frac1t-2t+C$.

Lecture example: $\int\frac{\cos x}{\sin^2x}\,dx$ (rewrite with trig)

$=\int\frac1{\sin x}\cdot\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\,dx=\int\csc x\cot x\,dx=-\csc x+C$.

Lecture example: $\int\frac{x^2}{x^2+1}\,dx$ (add and subtract)

$=\int\left(\frac{x^2+1}{x^2+1}-\frac1{x^2+1}\right)dx=\int\left(1-\frac1{x^2+1}\right)dx=x-\tan^{-1}x+C$.

3. Integral curves

Definition
The graphs of the antiderivatives of $f$ are the integral curves of $f$. If $y=F(x)$ is one integral curve, all others are its vertical translations $y=F(x)+C$. Example: integral curves of $x^2$ are $y=\frac13x^3+C$.
Lecture example: a curve with slope $x^2$ through $(2,1)$
  1. The slope of the tangent is $\frac{dy}{dx}=x^2$, so $y=\int x^2dx=\frac13x^3+C$.
  2. Through $(2,1)$: $1=\frac13(2^3)+C\Rightarrow C=-\frac53$.
  3. $y=\frac13x^3-\frac53$.

4. Differential equations and initial value problems

Definitions
An equation such as $\frac{dy}{dx}=x^2$ is a differential equation (it involves the derivative of an unknown function $y$). The problem $$\frac{dy}{dx}=f(x),\qquad y(x_0)=y_0$$ is an initial value problem: find the function with derivative $f$ whose graph passes through $(x_0,y_0)$. Its solution is the integral curve of $f$ through that point.
Lecture example: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\cos x$, $y(0)=1$

$y=\int\cos x\,dx=\sin x+C$; $1=\sin0+C\Rightarrow C=1$. Solution: $y=\sin x+1$.

5. More examples (end of Lecture 9)

(a) $\int\frac{\sec x+\cos x}{2\cos x}\,dx$

$=\frac12\int\sec^2x\,dx+\frac12\int dx=\frac12\tan x+\frac12x+C$.

(b) $\int\left[\frac1{2\sqrt{1-x^2}}-\frac3{1+x^2}\right]dx$

$=\frac12\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}-3\int\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\frac12\sin^{-1}x-3\tan^{-1}x+C$.

(c) $\int\left(\frac{10}{y^{3/4}}-3\sqrt[3]y+\frac4{\sqrt y}\right)dy$

$=\int\left(10y^{-3/4}-3y^{1/3}+4y^{-1/2}\right)dy=40y^{1/4}-\frac94y^{4/3}+8y^{1/2}+C$.

IVP: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x+1}{\sqrt x}$, $y(1)=0$
  1. $y=\int(x^{1/2}+x^{-1/2})\,dx=\frac23x^{3/2}+2x^{1/2}+C$.
  2. $0=\frac23+2+C\Rightarrow C=-\frac83$.
  3. $y(x)=\frac23x^{3/2}+2x^{1/2}-\frac83$, $x>0$.
IVP: $\frac{dy}{dt}=\sec^2t-\sin t$, $y(\frac\pi4)=1$
  1. $y(t)=\tan t+\cos t+C$.
  2. $1=\tan\frac\pi4+\cos\frac\pi4+C=1+\frac{\sqrt2}2+C\Rightarrow C=-\frac{\sqrt2}2$.
  3. $y(t)=\tan t+\cos t-\frac{\sqrt2}2$.
