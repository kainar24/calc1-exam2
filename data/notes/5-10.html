1. Where $\ln x$ comes from: equal areas under $y=\frac1t$

Problem. For $y=\frac1t$, $t\ge1$, find $t_1,t_2,t_3,\dots$ so that the areas $A_1=\int_1^{t_1},\ A_2=\int_{t_1}^{t_2},\dots$ are all equal.

  • $A_1=A_2$: $\int_1^{t_1}\frac{dt}t=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\frac{dt}t\Rightarrow\ln t_1=\ln t_2-\ln t_1\Rightarrow\ln t_2=2\ln t_1=\ln t_1^2\Rightarrow t_2=t_1^2$.
  • Similarly $t_3=t_1^3$, and in general $t_n=t_1^n$.
  • Choosing $t_1=e$ gives $t_n=e^n$ and every strip has area 1: $A_1=\int_1^e\frac{dt}t=1$, $A_2=\int_e^{e^2}\frac{dt}t=1$, …
  • So $\int_1^{e^n}\frac{dt}t=n=\ln(e^n)$. Replacing $e^n$ by any positive $x$: $\int_1^x\frac{dt}t=\ln x$.

2. The natural logarithm as an integral

Definition (natural logarithm)
$$\ln x=\int_1^x\frac1t\,dt,\qquad x>0.$$ For $x>1$ it is the area $A$ under $y=\frac1t$ from $1$ to $x$; for $0<x<1$ it is $\int_1^x=-\int_x^1\frac{dt}t=-A$. Hence $\ln x>0$ if $x>1$, $\ln x<0$ if $0<x<1$, $\ln1=0$.
Derivative (by FTC Part 2)
$\ln x$ is differentiable, hence continuous, on $(0,\infty)$, and $$\frac{d}{dx}[\ln x]=\frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_1^x\frac1t\,dt\right]=\frac1x\quad(x>0).$$ Since $\frac1x>0$, $\ln x$ is increasing (and $\frac{d^2}{dx^2}\ln x=-\frac1{x^2}<0$: concave down).
Theorem (algebraic properties)
For $a,c>0$ and rational $r$: (1) $\ln ac=\ln a+\ln c$; (2) $\ln\frac1c=-\ln c$; (3) $\ln\frac ac=\ln a-\ln c$; (4) $\ln a^r=r\ln a$.
Proof of (1): let $f(x)=\ln ax$. Then $f'(x)=\frac{a}{ax}=\frac1x$, so $\ln ax=\ln x+C$ (same derivative ⇒ differ by a constant). Put $x=1$: $C=\ln a$. Hence $\ln ax=\ln a+\ln x$.
(2): take $a=\frac1c$ in (1): $0=\ln1=\ln\frac1c+\ln c$. (3): $\ln\frac ac=\ln\left(a\cdot\frac1c\right)=\ln a-\ln c$. (4): induction, e.g. $\ln a^{n+1}=\ln a+\ln a^n=(n+1)\ln a$, then negative integers, then $r=\frac mn$.

3. $e$ and the exponential function

Definitions
  • $e$ is the unique solution of $\ln x=1$, i.e. $\int_1^e\frac{dt}t=1$.
  • The inverse of $\ln x$ is $e^x$, the natural exponential function.
  • For $a>0$ and real $r$: $a^r=e^{r\ln a}$.

4. Functions defined by integrals

Many important functions are not elementary (not polynomials, rational, exponential, log, trig, …). They come up when solving initial value problems.

Solution formula for an IVP
The solution of $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=f(x)$, $y(x_0)=y_0$ is $$y(x)=y_0+\int_{x_0}^xf(t)\,dt.$$ Check: $\frac{dy}{dx}=0+f(x)$ by the FTC, and $y(x_0)=y_0+0$.
Lecture example: $\frac{dy}{dx}=e^{-x^2}$, $y(0)=1$

$y(x)=1+\int_0^xe^{-t^2}dt$. This integral cannot be written with elementary functions. Related: the error function $\operatorname{erf}(x)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^xe^{-t^2}dt$, important in probability and statistics.

5. Differentiating integrals with variable limits

Formula
Let $F(x)=\int_a^xf(t)\,dt$, so $F(g(x))=\int_a^{g(x)}f(t)\,dt$. By the chain rule $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_a^{g(x)}f(t)\,dt\right]=F'(g(x))g'(x)=f(g(x))\,g'(x),$$ and in general $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_{h(x)}^{g(x)}f(t)\,dt\right]=f(g(x))g'(x)-f(h(x))h'(x).$$
Lecture examples
  • $\frac{d}{dx}\int_1^{\sin x}(1-t^2)\,dt=(1-\sin^2x)\cos x=\cos^3x$.
  • $\frac{d}{dx}\int_{x^2}^{\sin x}\sqrt{t^3+1}\,dt=\sqrt{\sin^3x+1}\,(\cos x)-\sqrt{x^6+1}\,(2x)$.
