1. Position and displacement

Definition
Motion of a particle along a straight line is called rectilinear motion. As the particle moves along the $s$-axis, its coordinate is a function of time, $s=s(t)$: the position function. The graph of $s$ against $t$ is the position versus time curve; where $s(t)>0$ the particle is on the positive side of the origin, where $s(t)<0$ it is on the negative side.
If $s(t_1)$ and $s(t_2)$ are the positions at times $t_1,t_2$, then $s(t_2)-s(t_1)$ is the displacement over $[t_1,t_2]$.
Lecture example: reading a position–time graph

The particle is at $s=-3$ when $t=0$. It moves in the positive direction until $t=4$ (since $s$ is increasing), reaching $s=3$. At $t=4$ it turns around and travels in the negative direction until $t=7$ (since $s$ is decreasing). At $t=7$ it is at $s=-1$ and stays there, since $s$ is constant for $t>7$.

2. Velocity, speed, acceleration

Definitions
Velocity: $v(t)=s'(t)=\dfrac{ds}{dt}$. Its sign tells the direction: $v>0$ ⇒ $s$ increasing (moving in the positive direction); $v<0$ ⇒ moving in the negative direction.
Speed: $|v(t)|=|s'(t)|=\left|\dfrac{ds}{dt}\right|$: how fast the particle moves; always $\ge0$, carries no direction.
Acceleration: $a(t)=v'(t)=\dfrac{dv}{dt}=s''(t)=\dfrac{d^2s}{dt^2}$.
Lecture example: $s(t)=t^3-6t^2$
  1. Position: $s(t)=t^2(t-6)$: negative side of the origin for $0<t<6$, positive side for $t>6$; at the origin at $t=0$ and $t=6$.
  2. Velocity: $v(t)=3t^2-12t=3t(t-4)$: moving in the negative direction for $0<t<4$, positive direction for $t>4$, momentarily stopped at $t=0$ and $t=4$.
  3. Speed: $|v(t)|=3t|t-4|$: increasing for $0<t<2$ and $t>4$, decreasing for $2<t<4$.
  4. Acceleration: $a(t)=6t-12=6(t-2)$.

3. Speeding up and slowing down

Remark (key rule)
A particle in rectilinear motion is speeding up when its velocity and acceleration have the same sign, and slowing down when they have opposite signs.
Lecture example (continued): $v=3t(t-4)$, $a=6(t-2)$

Same sign for $0<t<2$ (both negative) and for $t>4$ (both positive) ⇒ speeding up. Opposite signs for $2<t<4$ ($v<0$, $a>0$) ⇒ slowing down. This agrees with the speed graph above.

Lecture example: analyzing $s(t)=2t^3-21t^2+60t+3$
  1. $v(t)=6t^2-42t+60=6(t-2)(t-5)$; $a(t)=12t-42=12\left(t-\frac72\right)$.
  2. Sign lines: $v$: $+$ on $(0,2)$, $-$ on $(2,5)$, $+$ for $t>5$. $a$: $-$ before $\frac72$, $+$ after.
  3. Change in speed: slowing down on $(0,2)$, speeding up on $(2,\frac72)$, slowing down on $(\frac72,5)$, speeding up for $t>5$.
  4. The path on the $s$-axis: starts at $s(0)=3$, moves right to $s(2)=55$, turns around, passes $s(\frac72)=41.5$ (where it switches from speeding up to slowing down), stops at $s(5)=28$, turns around again and moves right forever.

Checklist for exam problems

  • “When is the particle at rest / does it change direction?” → solve $v=0$ and check that $v$ changes sign.
  • “Velocity when the acceleration is $k$” → solve $a(t)=k$ for $t$, then plug into $v$.
  • “Speeding up / slowing down” → two sign lines ($v$ and $a$) and compare.
  • “Maximum acceleration / velocity on $[t_0,t_1]$” → closed-interval method from §4.4 applied to $a$ or $v$.
