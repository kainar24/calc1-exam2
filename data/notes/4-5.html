A procedure for solving applied maximum and minimum problems (from the slides)
  1. Step 1. Draw an appropriate figure and label the quantities relevant to the problem.
  2. Step 2. Find a formula for the quantity to be maximized or minimized.
  3. Step 3. Using the conditions stated in the problem to eliminate variables, express the quantity to be maximized or minimized as a function of one variable.
  4. Step 4. Find the interval of possible values for this variable from the physical restrictions in the problem.
  5. Step 5. If applicable, use the techniques of §4.4 (absolute extrema) to obtain the maximum or minimum.
Step 4 decides which justification you use: a closed interval → compare values at critical points and endpoints; an open/infinite interval → use the limits at the ends (or the second derivative with a single critical point).

Model problem 1: the garden (closed interval)

A rectangular garden is fenced with 100 m of wire. What is the largest possible area?
  1. Let $x$ = length, $y$ = width (m), $A$ = area. Objective: $A=xy$.
  2. Constraint (perimeter): $2x+2y=100\Rightarrow y=50-x$. So $A(x)=x(50-x)=50x-x^2$.
  3. Interval of possible $x$: $0\le x\le50$ (lengths can't be negative; $y\ge0$). Goal: the absolute max of $A$ on $[0,50]$.
  4. $A'(x)=50-2x=2(25-x)$; critical point $x=25$.
  5. Compare: $A(0)=A(50)=0$, $A(25)=625$.
  6. Maximum area $625\ \text{m}^2$ when $x=25$ m (and then $y=25$ m: the best rectangle is a square).

Model problem 2: cylinder inscribed in a cone (closed interval)

Find the radius and height of the right circular cylinder of largest volume inscribed in a cone of radius 6 in and height 10 in.
  1. $r$ = radius of the cylinder, $h$ = its height, $V=\pi r^2h$.
  2. Similar triangles (cross-section through the axis): $\dfrac{10-h}{r}=\dfrac{10}{6}\Rightarrow h=10-\frac53r$.
  3. $V(r)=\pi r^2\left(10-\frac53r\right)=10\pi r^2-\frac53\pi r^3=\frac{5\pi r^2}{3}(6-r)$.
  4. Interval: $0\le r\le6$.
  5. $\dfrac{dV}{dr}=20\pi r-5\pi r^2=5\pi r(4-r)$; the only critical point in $(0,6)$ is $r=4$.
  6. $V(0)=V(6)=0$, $V(4)=\frac{5\pi\cdot16}{3}\cdot2=\frac{160}3\pi\approx168\ \text{in}^3$.
  7. Maximum volume $\frac{160}{3}\pi$ in³ at $r=4$ in, $h=10-\frac{20}3=\frac{10}3$ in.

Model problem 3: closest point (infinite interval)

Find the point on $y=x^2$ closest to $(18,0)$.
  1. $L$ = distance from $(18,0)$ to $(x,y)$ on the curve: $L=\sqrt{(x-18)^2+(y-0)^2}$.
  2. Since $y=x^2$: $L(x)=\sqrt{(x-18)^2+x^4}$, and there is no restriction on $x$: $x\in(-\infty,\infty)$.
  3. $\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}L(x)=+\infty$ ⇒ there is an absolute minimum but no absolute maximum.
  4. $L'(x)=\dfrac{2(x-18)+4x^3}{2L(x)}=\dfrac{2x^3+x-18}{L(x)}$. $L'=0\iff2x^3+x-18=0$; $x=2$ is the only real root ($2x^3+x-18=(x-2)(2x^2+4x+9)$ and the quadratic has no real roots).
  5. So the absolute minimum occurs at $x=2$: the point $(2,4)$ is the closest point.

Tip: minimizing $L^2=(x-18)^2+x^4$ instead of $L$ gives the same critical point with an easier derivative.

What to take from these three examples

  • Always write the objective as a function of one variable, using the constraint.
  • Always write the domain. It tells you how to justify max/min and earns a mark.
  • Closed domain: evaluate at the endpoints as well. Infinite/open domain: argue with limits (here $L\to\infty$), or with $f''$ at the single critical point.
  • Answer the exact question (dimensions? the point? the maximum value?) with units.
