What you need to know

  • Critical point: $f'(x_0)=0$ (stationary point) or $f'(x_0)$ does not exist, with $x_0$ in the domain.
  • First derivative test: $f'$ changes $+\to-$ ⇒ relative max; $-\to+$ ⇒ relative min; no change ⇒ neither.
  • Second derivative test: $f'(x_0)=0$ and $f''(x_0)>0$ ⇒ min; $f''(x_0)<0$ ⇒ max; $f''(x_0)=0$ ⇒ the test says nothing (use the first derivative test).
  • Graphing a polynomial: intercepts, end behaviour, critical points, inflection points, sign tables, then sketch.
Theorem 4.2.2 (relative extrema occur at critical points)
If $f$ has a relative extremum at $x_0$, then either $f'(x_0)=0$ or $f$ is not differentiable at $x_0$.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review Ch4 #2): $f(x)=(x^2+x)^2$
  1. $f'=2x(x+1)(2x+1)$ ⇒ stationary points $-1,-\frac12,0$.
  2. Signs: $-\,|\,+\,|\,-\,|\,+$.
  3. Rel. min $f(-1)=f(0)=0$; rel. max $f(-\frac12)=\frac1{16}$.
Example 2 (Castro, Fall 2018 Final): sketch $f(x)=2x+3x^{2/3}$

$f'=\frac{2(x^{1/3}+1)}{x^{1/3}}$: critical points $-1$ (stationary) and $0$ ($f'$ undefined). Rel. max $f(-1)=1$, rel. min $f(0)=0$ at a cusp. $f''=-\frac23x^{-4/3}<0$: concave down on both sides.

Common mistakes
  • Missing critical points where $f'$ does not exist.
  • Using the second derivative test when $f''(x_0)=0$ and concluding something.
Exam tips
  • Always give the extremum as a point or value: “relative max $\frac1{16}$ at $x=-\frac12$”.
