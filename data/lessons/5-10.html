No past problem yet (the section was taught in week 13). It may appear as an FTC-type derivative or as properties of $\ln$. Know the basics.

What you need to know

  • Definition: $\ln x=\displaystyle\int_1^x\frac{dt}t$ for $x>0$, the area under $y=\frac1t$ from 1 to $x$ (negative for $0<x<1$).
  • By FTC: $\frac{d}{dx}\ln x=\frac1x$; $\ln1=0$; $\ln$ is increasing and concave down.
  • $e$ is the number with $\int_1^e\frac{dt}t=1$.
  • General functions defined by integrals: $F(x)=\int_a^xf(t)\,dt$ has $F(a)=0$ and $F'(x)=f(x)$, so you can study it with §4.1–4.2 (increasing where $f>0$, inflection points where $f'$ changes sign).
  • Integrals with $\frac1x$: $\int\frac{dx}x=\ln|x|+C$, $\int_1^{a}\frac{dt}t=\ln a$.
Formulas
$\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b$, $\ \ln\frac ab=\ln a-\ln b$, $\ \ln a^r=r\ln a$ (all provable from the integral definition).

Examples

Example 1: where is $F(x)=\int_0^x\frac{t-1}{t^2+1}\,dt$ increasing, and where is its relative minimum?

$F'(x)=\frac{x-1}{x^2+1}$: negative for $x<1$, positive for $x>1$ ⇒ decreasing on $(-\infty,1]$, increasing on $[1,\infty)$, relative (and absolute) minimum at $x=1$.

Example 2: $\frac{d}{dx}\int_1^{x^2}\frac{dt}t$

$\frac1{x^2}\cdot2x=\frac2x$. Check: $\int_1^{x^2}\frac{dt}t=\ln x^2=2\ln x$, whose derivative is $\frac2x$. ✓
