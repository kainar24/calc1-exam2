9 past problems, usually short: “find $\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^{g(x)}f(t)\,dt$” or “evaluate $\int_a^bf$ with an antiderivative”.

What you need to know

  • FTC part 1 (evaluation): $\int_a^bf(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)$ for any antiderivative $F$ of a continuous $f$.
  • FTC part 2 (differentiation): $\dfrac{d}{dx}\int_a^xf(t)\,dt=f(x)$.
  • With a variable limit, use the chain rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\int_a^{g(x)}f(t)\,dt=f(g(x))\,g'(x)$.
  • Both limits variable: split at a constant, $\int_{h(x)}^{g(x)}=\int_c^{g(x)}-\int_c^{h(x)}$.
  • $f$ must be continuous on the interval. Do not apply the FTC across a discontinuity, e.g. $\int_{-1}^1\frac{dx}{x^2}$.

Key formulas

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $F'=f$, then $\displaystyle\int_a^bf(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)$.
If $f$ is continuous on an open interval containing $a$, then $\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^xf(t)\,dt=f(x)$.
Leibniz-type rule (what you actually use)
$$\frac{d}{dx}\int_{h(x)}^{g(x)}f(t)\,dt=f(g(x))\,g'(x)-f(h(x))\,h'(x).$$

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review #4a, Fall 2025 Test 3): $\frac{d}{dx}\int_1^{x^3}\sin^2t\,dt$

$f(t)=\sin^2t$, $g(x)=x^3$ ⇒ $\sin^2(x^3)\cdot3x^2=3x^2\sin^2(x^3)$.

Example 2 (Review #4b): $\frac{d}{dx}\int_{\sin x}^{x^2}\frac{dt}{1+t^2}$
  1. $\int_{\sin x}^{x^2}=\int_0^{x^2}-\int_0^{\sin x}$.
  2. $\dfrac1{1+x^4}\cdot2x-\dfrac1{1+\sin^2x}\cdot\cos x=\dfrac{2x}{1+x^4}-\dfrac{\cos x}{1+\sin^2x}$.
Example 3 (Castro, Fall 2020 Final): find $f$ and $a$ with $2+\int_a^xf(t)\,dt=e^{3x}$
  1. Differentiate: $f(x)=3e^{3x}$.
  2. Put $x=a$: $2+0=e^{3a}\Rightarrow a=\frac{\ln2}3$.
Example 4 (Fall 2024 MT3): $f(x)=\int_1^{2x}\sqrt{1+2t}\,dt$, find $f'(2)$

$f'(x)=\sqrt{1+4x}\cdot2$, so $f'(2)=2\cdot3=6$.

Common mistakes
  • Forgetting the factor $g'(x)$ from the chain rule. This is the most common lost mark.
  • Writing $f(x)$ instead of $f(g(x))$: you must substitute the upper limit into the integrand.
  • Trying to evaluate the integral first, when often it cannot be done ($\int\cos(t^2)\,dt$) and is not needed.
  • Sign errors when the variable is in the lower limit: $\frac{d}{dx}\int_x^5f=-f(x)$.
Exam tips
  • State the theorem you use (“by FTC Part 2 and the chain rule”): the instructions say you must cite results.
  • For $\int_a^b$ with an antiderivative, write $\left[F(x)\right]_a^b$ and then the two values. Partial credit comes from this line.
