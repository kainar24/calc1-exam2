5 past problems: area as a limit of right-endpoint sums (Review #1, Castro Fall 2020 MT3) and matching a $\lim\sum$ to an integral.

What you need to know

  • Equal subintervals: $\Delta x=\frac{b-a}n$, right endpoints $x_k^*=a+k\Delta x$, left $a+(k-1)\Delta x$, midpoints $a+(k-\frac12)\Delta x$.
  • $A=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^nf(x_k^*)\Delta x$.
  • Expand $f(x_k^*)$, pull constants out of the sum, use the closed formulas, then let $n\to\infty$.
  • Reverse direction: from $\sum f\left(a+\frac{(b-a)k}n\right)\frac{b-a}n$, read off $\Delta x$, $a$, $b$ and $f$.
Sum formulas (Theorem 5.4.2)
$$\sum_{k=1}^n1=n,\quad\sum_{k=1}^nk=\frac{n(n+1)}2,\quad\sum_{k=1}^nk^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6,\quad\sum_{k=1}^nk^3=\left[\frac{n(n+1)}2\right]^2$$

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review Ch5 #1): area under $y=1-x^2$ on $[0,1]$, right endpoints
  1. $\Delta x=\frac1n$, $x_k^*=\frac kn$, $f(x_k^*)=1-\frac{k^2}{n^2}$.
  2. $A_n=\frac1n\sum1-\frac1{n^3}\sum k^2=1-\frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^2}$.
  3. $A=1-\frac26=\frac23$.
Example 2 (Castro, Fall 2020 MT3): $\int_0^3(x^3-6x)\,dx$ by a Riemann sum

$\Delta x=\frac3n$, $x_k=\frac{3k}n$: $\frac{81}{n^4}\sum k^3-\frac{54}{n^2}\sum k=\frac{81}4\left(1+\frac1n\right)^2-27\left(1+\frac1n\right)\to-\frac{27}4$.

Example 3 (Fall 2024 MT3): which integral is $\lim\sum\left[\left(1+\frac{2i}n\right)^2+1\right]\frac2n$?

$\Delta x=\frac2n$, start $a=1$, so $b=3$, $f(x)=x^2+1$: $\int_1^3(x^2+1)\,dx$.

Common mistakes
  • $x_k^*=k\Delta x$ when $a\ne0$. It must be $a+k\Delta x$.
  • Forgetting the factor $\Delta x$ in the sum.
  • Taking $n\to\infty$ before simplifying, which gives forms like $\infty-\infty$.
Exam tips
  • Check your limit against the FTC answer on the side (e.g. $\int_0^1(1-x^2)dx=\frac23$). If they differ, find the slip.
