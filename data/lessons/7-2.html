6 past problems, and Chapter 7 is explicitly on Exam 2. Our instructor's review starts with three integration-by-parts problems.

What you need to know

  • Formula: $\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du$. Definite: $\int_a^bu\,dv=\left[uv\right]_a^b-\int_a^bv\,du$.
  • Choose $u$ by LIATE (Log, Inverse trig, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential): the earlier in the list, the better a choice for $u$.
  • Polynomial × $\sin$/$\cos$/$e^{x}$: use the tabular method (differentiate the polynomial to 0).
  • $\ln x$, $\arctan x$ alone: take $u=\ln x$ (or $\arctan x$), $dv=dx$.
  • “Cyclic” integrals ($e^x\sin x$, $\cos(\ln x)$): integrate by parts twice, then solve for the integral.
  • Sometimes substitute first ($\int x^5e^{x^2}dx$: put $y=x^2$, then use parts).
Tabular method (D / I columns)
Differentiate $u$ down to $0$ in column D, integrate $dv$ in column I, multiply along the diagonals with signs $+,-,+,-,\dots$ and add.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review Ch7 #1b): $\int_1^ex^2\ln x\,dx$
  1. $u=\ln x$, $dv=x^2dx$ ⇒ $du=\frac{dx}x$, $v=\frac{x^3}3$.
  2. $\left[\frac{x^3}3\ln x\right]_1^e-\frac13\int_1^ex^2dx=\frac{e^3}3-\frac{e^3-1}9=\frac{2e^3+1}9$.
Example 2 (Review Ch7 #1a): $\int4x^4\sin2x\,dx$ (tabular)
D$4x^4$$16x^3$$48x^2$$96x$$96$$0$
I$\sin2x$$-\frac12\cos2x$$-\frac14\sin2x$$\frac18\cos2x$$\frac1{16}\sin2x$$-\frac1{32}\cos2x$

$=-2x^4\cos2x+4x^3\sin2x+6x^2\cos2x-6x\sin2x-3\cos2x+C$.

Example 3 (Review Ch7 #2): $\int\cos(\ln x)\,dx$ (cyclic)
  1. $u=\cos(\ln x)$, $dv=dx$: $I=x\cos(\ln x)+\int\sin(\ln x)\,dx$.
  2. $u=\sin(\ln x)$, $dv=dx$: $\int\sin(\ln x)\,dx=x\sin(\ln x)-I$.
  3. $2I=x(\cos\ln x+\sin\ln x)$ ⇒ $I=\frac x2(\cos\ln x+\sin\ln x)+C$.
Example 4 (Fall 2024 MT3): $\int_0^1x\arctan x\,dx$

$u=\arctan x$, $v=\frac{x^2}2$: $\frac\pi8-\frac12\int_0^1\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}dx=\frac\pi8-\frac12\left(1-\frac\pi4\right)=\frac\pi4-\frac12$.

Common mistakes
  • Choosing $u=e^x$ or $u=\sin x$ when the other factor is a polynomial: the integral gets worse.
  • Sign errors in the tabular method: the signs alternate starting with $+$.
  • In cyclic problems, writing $I=\dots+I$ and “cancelling”. The two $I$ must have opposite signs; solve $2I=\dots$.
  • Forgetting to evaluate $uv$ at both limits in definite problems.
Exam tips
  • Write the $u,dv,du,v$ box explicitly: it earns method points.
  • For $\int x^n\ln x\,dx$, always $u=\ln x$. For $\int x^ne^{ax}dx$, always tabular.
