6 past problems: closed-interval method, plus “does it have absolute extrema on an open interval?” (Review Ch4 #3) and theory questions.

What you need to know

  • Extreme Value Theorem: continuous on a closed, bounded $[a,b]$ ⇒ absolute max and min both exist.
  • Closed-interval method: (1) find the critical points in $(a,b)$, where $f'=0$ or $f'$ does not exist; (2) evaluate $f$ there and at $a,b$; (3) the largest value is the max, the smallest is the min.
  • On open or infinite intervals, extrema may not exist. Look at the limits at the ends. If there is a single critical point that is a relative extremum, it is absolute (Theorem 4.4.4).
  • Discontinuous functions may fail to have extrema even on $[a,b]$.
Theorem 4.4.2 (EVT) and 4.4.3
If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, it has an absolute max and min there. If an absolute extremum occurs inside $(a,b)$, it occurs at a critical point.
Theorem 4.4.4 (single critical point)
If $f$ is continuous on an interval and has exactly one relative extremum there at $x_0$, then a relative max is the absolute max and a relative min is the absolute min.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Fall 2022 Exam 3): $f(x)=x^3-3x^2+1$ on $[-\frac12,4]$

$f'=3x(x-2)$ ⇒ $x=0,2$. Values: $f(-\frac12)=\frac18$, $f(0)=1$, $f(2)=-3$, $f(4)=17$. Max $17$ at $x=4$, min $-3$ at $x=2$.

Example 2 (Castro, Fall 2017 MT2): $f(x)=3x^{2/3}-2x$ on $[-1,1]$

$f'=2x^{-1/3}-2$: zero at $x=1$, undefined at $x=0$. Values $f(-1)=5$, $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$. Max $5$, min $0$. Don't forget the point where $f'$ does not exist!

Example 3 (Review Ch4 #3): $f(x)=\frac1{x^3-3x^2}$ on $(0,3)$
  1. $f=\frac1{x^2(x-3)}<0$ and $f\to-\infty$ at both ends ⇒ no absolute minimum.
  2. $f'=-\frac{3x(x-2)}{(x^3-3x^2)^2}$: only critical point $x=2$; $f\nearrow$ on $(0,2)$, $\searrow$ on $(2,3)$.
  3. Absolute max $f(2)=-\frac14$.
Example 4 (Castro, Fall 2017 Final): $x-\tan x$ on $[-\frac\pi4,\frac\pi4]$

$f'=-\tan^2x\le0$ ⇒ decreasing. Max $1-\frac\pi4$ at the left end, min $\frac\pi4-1$ at the right end.

Common mistakes
  • Forgetting the endpoints, or including critical points outside the interval.
  • Missing critical points where $f'$ is undefined ($x^{2/3}$, $|x|$).
  • Giving only the location ($x=2$) or only the value. Give both: “abs max value 17 at $x=4$”.
  • Claiming extrema exist on an open interval without checking the end behaviour.
Exam tips
  • Put the candidates in a small table $x\mid f(x)$; graders like it.
  • Say “$f$ is continuous on the closed interval, so by the EVT…” before computing.
