7 past problems: properties ($\int_a^b=\int_a^c+\int_c^b$), integrals of $|x-a|$ and piecewise functions, integrals as signed area, and the limit definition.

What you need to know

  • Definition: $\displaystyle\int_a^bf(x)\,dx=\lim_{\max\Delta x_k\to0}\sum_{k=1}^nf(x_k^*)\Delta x_k$, i.e. the net signed area: area above the axis minus area below.
  • Continuous functions (and functions with finitely many jumps) are integrable.
  • Many exam integrals are solved by geometry: triangles for $|x-a|$, trapezoids for lines, quarter or half circles for $\sqrt{r^2-x^2}$.
  • For an absolute value or piecewise function, split at the break points.

Properties (Theorem 5.5.4–5.5.5)

$\int_a^af=0,\qquad \int_b^af=-\int_a^bf,\qquad \int_a^bf=\int_a^cf+\int_c^bf$ (any order of $a,b,c$)
$\int_a^b(cf\pm g)=c\int_a^bf\pm\int_a^bg,\qquad \int_a^bc\,dx=c(b-a)$
If $f\ge0$ on $[a,b]$, then $\int_a^bf\ge0$; if $f\le g$, then $\int_a^bf\le\int_a^bg$.
Odd $f$: $\int_{-a}^af=0$.  Even $f$: $\int_{-a}^af=2\int_0^af$.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Fall 2024 MT3 #1): $\int_1^8f=12$, $\int_5^1f=3$, $\int_7^8f=4$; find $\int_5^7(2f-1)\,dx$
  1. $\int_1^8=\int_1^5+\int_5^7+\int_7^8$, and $\int_1^5f=-3$.
  2. $12=-3+\int_5^7f+4\Rightarrow\int_5^7f=11$.
  3. $\int_5^7(2f-1)\,dx=2(11)-(7-5)=20$.
Example 2 (Review #3a): piecewise $f$, $\int_0^4f$

$f=5-x$ for $x\le2$, $x^2$ for $x>2$: $\int_0^2(5-x)\,dx+\int_2^4x^2\,dx=8+\frac{56}3=\frac{80}3$.

Example 3 (Castro, Fall 2017 Final): $\int_{-3}^4(|x+1|-|x-1|+|x+2|)\,dx$

Use triangles for each term: $14.5-12.5+18.5=\frac{41}2$. (E.g. $\int_{-3}^4|x+1|\,dx$: two triangles with legs $2$ and $5$: $\frac{2^2}2+\frac{5^2}2$.)

Example 4 (Fall 2025 Test 3): write $\int_0^3\frac{x}{9x+8}dx$ as a limit of Riemann sums

$\displaystyle\lim_{\max\Delta x_k\to0}\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{x_k^*}{9x_k^*+8}\Delta x_k$. Watch the details: the sum goes to $n$, not to 3, and keeps $\Delta x_k$.

Common mistakes
  • Treating $\int_5^1f$ as $\int_1^5f$: reversing the limits changes the sign.
  • $\int(2f-1)=2\int f-1$ is wrong: the constant also integrates, giving $-(b-a)$.
  • For $\int|g|$, not splitting at the zero of $g$.
Exam tips
  • Draw the graph. A 10-second sketch of $|x+1|$ turns the integral into two triangles.
  • Write the additivity equation explicitly before plugging in numbers.
