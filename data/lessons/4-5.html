6 past problems, usually worth 12–13 points: the open-top box with different material costs (Fall 2025 Test 2, both versions), the cylinder can, rectangles under curves, triangles cut off by tangent lines.

What you need to know: the 6-step recipe

  1. Draw a picture and name the variables.
  2. Write the quantity to optimize (cost, area, perimeter, distance).
  3. Use the constraint (volume, curve equation) to reduce it to one variable.
  4. State the domain (e.g. $w>0$, $0<x\le1$).
  5. Find the critical points of the one-variable function.
  6. Justify that it is a max/min: closed interval → compare values at critical points and endpoints; open interval → second derivative test plus behaviour at the ends (or a sign chart).
Formulas that come up
Box with square or rectangular base $\ell\times w$, height $h$: $V=\ell wh$; open top: area $=\ell w+2\ell h+2wh$.
Cylinder: $V=\pi r^2h$, closed surface $S=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$.
Distance from $(x,y)$ to $(a,b)$: minimize $D^2=(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2$ instead of $D$.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Fall 2025 Test 2, version A, 13 pts): open box, $V=8$, $\ell=2w$, base 3 dollars per m², sides 4 dollars per m²
  1. $h=\dfrac{8}{2w^2}=\dfrac4{w^2}$.
  2. Cost $C=3(2w^2)+4(2wh+2\ell h)=6w^2+24wh=6w^2+\dfrac{96}w$, $w>0$.
  3. $C'(w)=12w-\dfrac{96}{w^2}=0\Rightarrow w^3=8\Rightarrow w=2$.
  4. $C''(w)=12+\dfrac{192}{w^3}>0$ ⇒ minimum; it is the only critical point and $C\to\infty$ at both ends, so it is the absolute minimum.
  5. Width $2$ m (length 4, height 1, cost 72).
Example 2 (Review Ch4 #4): rectangle under $y=1/x$ with smallest perimeter

$P(x)=2x+\frac2x$, $P'=2-\frac2{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x=1$, $P''=\frac4{x^3}>0$. So $P(1,1)$ and perimeter $4$.

Example 3 (Castro, Fall 2020 MT3): one-liter cylinder can with least material

$h=\frac{1000}{\pi r^2}$, $S=2\pi r^2+\frac{2000}r$, $S'=4\pi r-\frac{2000}{r^2}=0\Rightarrow r=\sqrt[3]{500/\pi}\approx5.42$ cm, and $h=2r$.

Example 4 (Castro, Fall 2018 Final): tangent to $y=1-x^2$ that cuts off the smallest triangle

Tangent at $a$: $y=1+a^2-2ax$; area $A=\frac{(1+a^2)^2}{4a}$, $A'=\frac{(1+a^2)(3a^2-1)}{4a^2}=0\Rightarrow a=\frac1{\sqrt3}$.

Common mistakes
  • Not stating the domain, so you can't justify an absolute extremum.
  • Open top: counting the top in the area/cost.
  • Answering a different quantity than asked (they asked for the width, not the cost).
  • Skipping the justification (“show that your answer is a minimum”). That costs points.
Exam tips
  • The official marking follows the steps: set up variables, constraint, objective in one variable, derivative, critical point, justification, answer.
  • Keep exact values ($\sqrt[3]{18}$); a decimal approximation is optional.
