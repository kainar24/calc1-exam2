Second most frequent section (9 problems). It often comes as “read the graph of $f'$” or as a full analysis: increasing/decreasing, concavity, inflection points.

What you need to know

  • $f'>0$ on an interval ⇒ $f$ increasing; $f'<0$ ⇒ decreasing.
  • $f''>0$ ⇒ concave up (cup, $f'$ increasing); $f''<0$ ⇒ concave down (cap, $f'$ decreasing).
  • Inflection point: a point where $f$ is continuous and the concavity changes. $f''=0$ alone is not enough; the sign must change.
  • Method: compute $f'$ and $f''$, factor them, find zeros and undefined points, then make a sign table.
  • From a graph of $f'$: $f$ increases where the graph of $f'$ is above the axis. $f$ is concave up where the graph of $f'$ is going up.

Key facts

Theorem 4.1.2 (monotonicity)
Let $f$ be continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$. If $f'(x)>0$ for all $x\in(a,b)$, then $f$ is increasing on $[a,b]$; if $f'(x)<0$, decreasing; if $f'(x)=0$, constant.
Theorem 4.1.4 (concavity)
If $f''(x)>0$ on an open interval, $f$ is concave up there; if $f''(x)<0$, concave down.
Useful derivatives
$\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{g(x)}=g'(x)e^{g(x)}$, $\quad\dfrac{d}{dx}\dfrac{\ln x}{x}=\dfrac{1-\ln x}{x^2}$, $\quad\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(xe^{-x^2}\right)=(1-2x^2)e^{-x^2}$.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review Ch4 #1): $f(x)=e^{-x^2/2}$
  1. $f'(x)=-xe^{-x^2/2}$. Since $e^{(\cdot)}>0$, the sign of $f'$ is the sign of $-x$: increasing on $(-\infty,0]$, decreasing on $[0,\infty)$.
  2. $f''(x)=-e^{-x^2/2}+x^2e^{-x^2/2}=(x^2-1)e^{-x^2/2}$, zero at $x=\pm1$.
  3. Sign table for $x^2-1$: $+\,|\,-\,|\,+$ ⇒ concave up on $(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,\infty)$, concave down on $(-1,1)$.
  4. Inflection points: $(\pm1,\,e^{-1/2})=(\pm1,\tfrac1{\sqrt e})$.
Example 2 (Fall 2025 Test 2): reading the graph of $f'$

The graph of $f'(x)=(x+2)(x-1)^2$ is given. It crosses zero at $x=-2$, has a peak at $x=-1$ and only touches zero at $x=1$.

  • Increasing: $f'\ge0$ ⇒ $[-2,\infty)$. At $x=1$ the sign does not change, so it is not an extremum.
  • Concave down: $f'$ decreasing ⇒ $(-1,1)$. Concave up: $(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,\infty)$.
Example 3 (Fall 2024 MT2): inflection points of $y=2x+\cos x$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$

$y''=-\cos x=0$ at $x=\pm\frac\pi2$, and $-\cos x$ changes sign at both ⇒ 2 inflection points.

Common mistakes
  • Calling every zero of $f''$ an inflection point. Always check that the sign changes ($y=x^4$ has $f''(0)=0$ but no inflection).
  • Mixing up the graphs of $f$ and $f'$. Read the question carefully: “the graph below shows $f'$”.
  • Forgetting points where $f'$ or $f''$ is undefined (e.g. $x^{1/3}$).
  • Giving inflection points as $x$-values only, when the question asks for points $(x,f(x))$.
Exam tips
  • Always factor $f'$ and $f''$ completely. Exponentials are always positive, so drop them from the sign analysis.
  • Draw the sign table: it is worth marks even if you make an arithmetic slip.
  • Intervals of increase can be written closed, $[a,b]$; concavity intervals open.
