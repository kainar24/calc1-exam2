What you need to know (strategy table)

$\int\sin^mx\cos^nx\,dx$
  • $n$ odd: save one $\cos x$, convert the rest with $\cos^2=1-\sin^2$, put $u=\sin x$.
  • $m$ odd: save one $\sin x$, convert with $\sin^2=1-\cos^2$, put $u=\cos x$.
  • Both even: use the half-angle formulas $\sin^2x=\frac{1-\cos2x}2$, $\cos^2x=\frac{1+\cos2x}2$.
$\int\tan^mx\sec^nx\,dx$
  • $n$ even: save $\sec^2x$, convert the rest with $\sec^2=1+\tan^2$, put $u=\tan x$.
  • $m$ odd: save $\sec x\tan x$, convert with $\tan^2=\sec^2-1$, put $u=\sec x$.
  • Only $\tan^mx$: split off $\tan^2x=\sec^2x-1$ repeatedly.
Must-know integrals and identities
$\int\tan x\,dx=\ln|\sec x|+C$, $\ \int\sec x\,dx=\ln|\sec x+\tan x|+C$
$\sin A\cos B=\frac12[\sin(A-B)+\sin(A+B)]$, $\ \sin A\sin B=\frac12[\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)]$, $\ \cos A\cos B=\frac12[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)]$

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review Ch7 #3): $\int_0^{\pi/3}\sin^43x\cos^33x\,dx$

Odd cosine: $\sin^43x(1-\sin^23x)\cos3x$, $u=\sin3x$; limits $0\to\sin\pi=0$ ⇒ the integral is $0$.

Example 2 (Review Ch7 #4): $\int\tan^4x\,dx$

$\tan^4x=\tan^2x\sec^2x-\sec^2x+1$ ⇒ $\frac{\tan^3x}3-\tan x+x+C$.

Example 3 (Review Ch7 #5): $\int\sqrt{\tan x}\sec^4x\,dx$

Even secant: $\sqrt{\tan x}(1+\tan^2x)\sec^2x$, $u=\tan x$: $\frac23\tan^{3/2}x+\frac27\tan^{7/2}x+C$.

Example 4 (both even): $\int\cos^2x\,dx$

$\int\frac{1+\cos2x}2dx=\frac x2+\frac{\sin2x}4+C$.

Common mistakes
  • Saving the wrong factor (with $m$ odd, you must save $\sin x$, not $\cos x$).
  • Forgetting the chain-rule factor: $u=\sin3x$ gives $du=3\cos3x\,dx$.
  • Mixing up $\sec^2=1+\tan^2$ and $\tan^2=\sec^2-1$.
Exam tips
  • First decide which power is odd or even and write the plan (“save $\cos x$, $u=\sin x$”). The rest is mechanical.
