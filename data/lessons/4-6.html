4 past problems: velocity when acceleration equals a value (Fall 2025 Test 2), maximum acceleration on an interval, speeding up / slowing down (Lecture 8).

What you need to know

  • $v(t)=s'(t)$, $a(t)=v'(t)=s''(t)$, speed $=|v(t)|$.
  • $v>0$: moving in the positive direction; $v<0$: negative; $v=0$: momentarily stopped (a possible turning point).
  • Speeding up when $v$ and $a$ have the same sign; slowing down when they have opposite signs.
  • Max or min of $v$ or $a$ on an interval: use the closed-interval method of §4.4.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Fall 2025 Test 2 A): $s=\frac12t^3-3t^2+4t$; $v$ when $a=6$

$a=3t-6=6\Rightarrow t=4$; $v(4)=24-24+4=4$ m/s.

Example 2 (Fall 2024 MT2 #14): maximum acceleration on $[0,3]$ for $v=\frac23t^3-4t^2+8t-2$

$a=2t^2-8t+8=2(t-2)^2$; candidates $a(0)=8$, $a(2)=0$, $a(3)=2$ ⇒ max $8$ at $t=0$.

Example 3 (Lecture 8): $s(t)=2t^3-21t^2+60t+3$

$v=6(t-2)(t-5)$, $a=12\left(t-\frac72\right)$. Speeding up on $(2,\frac72)$ and $(5,\infty)$, slowing down on $(0,2)$ and $(\frac72,5)$. It turns around at $t=2$ ($s=55$) and at $t=5$ ($s=28$).

Interactive: $s$, $v$, $a$

Common mistakes
  • Confusing speed with velocity (speed is never negative).
  • “Speeding up when $a>0$”: not always! Compare the signs of $v$ and $a$.
Exam tips
  • Draw two sign lines (for $v$ and $a$) one above the other; the speeding-up intervals can be read off directly.
