5 past problems: position from acceleration with two conditions, displacement vs distance, average velocity.

What you need to know

  • $s'(t)=v(t)$, $v'(t)=a(t)$. Going back means integrating: $v=\int a\,dt$, $s=\int v\,dt$. Each step adds a constant.
  • Displacement on $[t_0,t_1]$: $\int_{t_0}^{t_1}v(t)\,dt=s(t_1)-s(t_0)$ (signed).
  • Distance traveled: $\int_{t_0}^{t_1}|v(t)|\,dt$. Split where $v$ changes sign.
  • Two conditions (e.g. $s(1)$ and $s(3)$) give two equations for $C_1, C_2$.
  • Constant acceleration $a$: $v=v_0+at$, $s=s_0+v_0t+\frac12at^2$.
Formulas
$$\text{displacement}=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}v\,dt,\qquad\text{distance}=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}|v|\,dt,\qquad v_{\text{ave}}=\frac{1}{t_1-t_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_1}v\,dt$$

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review Ch5 #6): $v(t)=\cos t$, $0\le t\le\pi$

Displacement $[\sin t]_0^\pi=0$. Distance: $\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos t\,dt-\int_{\pi/2}^\pi\cos t\,dt=1+1=2$ m.

Example 2 (Fall 2025 Test 3): $a(t)=-\frac4{t^2}$, $s(1)=2$, $s(3)=4\ln3$
  1. $v=\frac4t+C_1$, $s=4\ln t+C_1t+C_2$.
  2. $C_1+C_2=2$ and $4\ln3+3C_1+C_2=4\ln3$.
  3. $C_1=-1$, $C_2=3$ ⇒ $s(t)=4\ln t-t+3$.
Example 3 (Fall 2024 MT3): from rest, $a=2$; average velocity on $[1,5]$

$v=2t$, $v_{\text{ave}}=\frac14\int_1^52t\,dt=\frac{25-1}4=6$ m/s.

Common mistakes
  • Computing distance as $\left|\int v\,dt\right|$. You need $\int|v|\,dt$.
  • Using one constant for two integrations.
  • Not finding where $v=0$ before splitting.
Exam tips
  • Make a sign table of $v$ first, then write distance as a sum of $\pm\int v$ over the pieces.
