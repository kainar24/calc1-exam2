7 past problems. Indefinite substitution also appears inside average value, initial value problems and integration by parts.

What you need to know

  • Look for a function and its derivative (up to a constant) in the integrand. Let $u$ be the “inside” function.
  • Steps: choose $u$, compute $du$, rewrite everything in $u$ (no $x$ left), integrate, substitute back, add $+C$.
  • If an extra $x$ is left over, express it with $u$, e.g. $x^2=u-1$ when $u=x^2+1$.
  • Check by differentiating your answer.

Key formulas

$\int f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx=\int f(u)\,du$ with $u=g(x)$.
Patterns: $\int\frac{g'}{g}=\ln|g|+C$; $\int g^ng'=\frac{g^{n+1}}{n+1}+C$; $\int\frac{g'}{1+g^2}=\arctan g+C$; $\int\frac{g'}{\sqrt{1-g^2}}=\arcsin g+C$; $\int\frac{dx}{a^2+x^2}=\frac1a\arctan\frac xa+C$.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review #3b): $\displaystyle\int\frac{x^5+1}{\sqrt{x^6+6x}}\,dx$
  1. $u=x^6+6x$, $du=6(x^5+1)\,dx$.
  2. $\frac16\int u^{-1/2}du=\frac13\sqrt u+C=\frac13\sqrt{x^6+6x}+C$.
Example 2 (Fall 2025 Test 3): $\displaystyle\int\frac{4x^3}{9+x^8}\,dx$

$u=x^4$, $du=4x^3dx$: $\int\frac{du}{9+u^2}=\frac13\arctan\frac u3+C=\frac13\arctan\frac{x^4}3+C$.

Example 3 (Castro, Fall 2020 MT3): $\displaystyle\int x^5\sqrt{1+x^2}\,dx$
  1. $u=1+x^2$, $du=2x\,dx$, $x^4=(u-1)^2$.
  2. $\frac12\int(u-1)^2u^{1/2}du=\frac12\int(u^{5/2}-2u^{3/2}+u^{1/2})\,du$.
  3. $=\frac17u^{7/2}-\frac25u^{5/2}+\frac13u^{3/2}+C$, $u=1+x^2$.
Example 4 (Fall 2024 MT2): $\displaystyle\int\frac{x^3}{\sqrt{1-x^8}}\,dx$

$u=x^4$: $\frac14\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}=\frac14\arcsin(x^4)+C$.

Common mistakes
  • Leaving some $x$ in the $u$-integral (“$\int u^3x\,du$”). That integral is meaningless.
  • Losing the constant factor from $du$ (e.g. the $\frac16$ above).
  • Forgetting $+C$, or forgetting to substitute back.
  • $\int\frac{du}{9+u^2}=\arctan\frac u3$ without the $\frac13$ in front.
Exam tips
  • When you see $x^{n-1}$ next to an $x^n$ inside something, take $u=x^n$.
  • Differentiate your final answer: it takes 30 seconds and catches most errors.
