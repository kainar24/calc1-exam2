No past problem tests §5.1 alone; it is the idea behind §5.4–5.6. Learn it quickly.

What you need to know

  • Rectangle method: approximate the area under $y=f(x)\ge0$ on $[a,b]$ by $n$ rectangles of width $\frac{b-a}n$; as $n\to\infty$ the sum tends to the exact area.
  • Lower/upper sums: rectangles inside / containing the region. Midpoints are usually more accurate.
  • Antiderivative method: if $A(x)$ is the area from $a$ to $x$, then $A'(x)=f(x)$. This is the seed of the FTC.
Key idea
If $f$ is continuous and nonnegative on $[a,b]$ and $A(x)$ is the area under $f$ over $[a,x]$, then $A'(x)=f(x)$ and $A(a)=0$.

Example (Lecture 9)

Area under $y=x^2$ on $[0,1]$ with $n$ right-endpoint rectangles

$\sum_{k=1}^n\left(\frac kn\right)^2\frac1n=\frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^2}\to\frac13$. With the antiderivative method: $A(x)=\frac{x^3}3$, so $A(1)=\frac13$.

Exam tip
  • For an increasing $f$, left endpoints undershoot and right endpoints overshoot (the other way round for decreasing $f$).
