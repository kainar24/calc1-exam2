7 past problems: “find $c$ in the MVT”, “why the MVT does not apply”, and proofs of inequalities with the MVT (Castro bonus problems, Fall 2020 MT3).

What you need to know

  • Hypotheses (check both and say them aloud on the exam): $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$.
  • Rolle: if moreover $f(a)=f(b)$, there is $c\in(a,b)$ with $f'(c)=0$.
  • MVT: there is $c\in(a,b)$ with $f'(c)=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$: the tangent at $c$ is parallel to the secant.
  • To find $c$: compute the secant slope, solve $f'(c)=\text{slope}$, keep only the solutions inside $(a,b)$.
  • Inequalities: apply the MVT on $[x,y]$ (or $[0,x]$) and bound $f'(c)$ using $x<c<y$.
  • Typical failures: corners ($|x-3|$), cusps ($x^{2/3}$ at 0), discontinuities, $c$ at an endpoint.

Theorems

Rolle's Theorem (4.8.1)
If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$ and $f(a)=f(b)=0$ (in general $f(a)=f(b)$), then there is at least one $c\in(a,b)$ with $f'(c)=0$.
Mean-Value Theorem (4.8.2)
If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, then there is at least one $c\in(a,b)$ with $$f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}.$$ Consequences: if $f'=0$ on an interval, $f$ is constant; if $f'=g'$, then $f=g+C$.

Worked examples

Example 1 (Review Ch4 #5): $f(x)=x+\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$
  1. Continuous and differentiable everywhere ⇒ MVT applies.
  2. Secant slope $=\frac{\pi-0}{\pi-0}=1$.
  3. $f'(c)=1+\cos c=1\Rightarrow\cos c=0\Rightarrow c=\frac\pi2\in(0,\pi)$.
Example 2 (Fall 2025 Test 2): $f(x)=x^2-2x$ on $[-3,5]$

Slope $\frac{15-15}{8}=0$, $f'(c)=2c-2=0\Rightarrow c=1$.

Example 3 (Castro, Fall 2020 MT3): show $\sqrt{1+x}<1+\frac x2$ for $x>0$
  1. $f(t)=\sqrt{1+t}$ on $[0,x]$ satisfies the hypotheses.
  2. MVT: $\dfrac{\sqrt{1+x}-1}{x}=\dfrac1{2\sqrt{1+c}}$ for some $0<c<x$.
  3. $c>0\Rightarrow\sqrt{1+c}>1\Rightarrow\dfrac1{2\sqrt{1+c}}<\dfrac12$.
  4. So $\sqrt{1+x}-1<\frac x2$. ∎
Example 4 (Fall 2024 MT2): why the MVT fails for $|x-3|$ on $[1,4]$

Not differentiable at $x=3\in(1,4)$ (corner). Indeed $f'=\pm1$ never equals the secant slope $-\frac13$.

Common mistakes
  • Not checking (or not writing) the hypotheses. They are usually worth a point.
  • Keeping a $c$ outside $(a,b)$, or forgetting a second valid $c$.
  • In proofs: writing $c<x$ when actually $x<c$. Draw the interval and mark where $c$ lies.
Exam tips
  • Template for inequality proofs: choose $f$, choose the interval, check the hypotheses, write the MVT equation, bound $f'(c)$, rearrange.
  • If the answer for $c$ is ugly, check it numerically: it must lie strictly between $a$ and $b$.
