Most frequent topic on past papers. 12 in-scope problems (Fall 2025 Test 3, Fall 2022 Exam 3, Fall 2024 MT3, Castro finals, and our instructor's review).

What you need to know

  • A definite integral $\int_a^b f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx$ is computed with the substitution $u=g(x)$.
  • There are two correct ways: (1) change the limits to $u=g(a)$, $u=g(b)$ and never go back to $x$; (2) find the antiderivative in $u$, substitute back to $x$, then use the old limits.
  • Method (1) is faster and is what the official solutions use. You get marks for writing the new limits.
  • After substituting, look for something you can recognize: a quarter or half disk $\int\sqrt{r^2-u^2}\,du$, $\arcsin$, $\arctan$, or the power rule.
  • If both new limits are equal, the integral is $0$. If the upper limit becomes smaller than the lower one, keep the order and the sign takes care of itself.

Key formulas

Substitution rule for definite integrals
$$\int_a^b f(g(x))\,g'(x)\,dx=\int_{g(a)}^{g(b)}f(u)\,du.$$
Integrals you must recognize after substituting
$$\int_0^r\sqrt{r^2-u^2}\,du=\frac{\pi r^2}{4},\qquad \int_{-r}^{r}\sqrt{r^2-u^2}\,du=\frac{\pi r^2}{2}$$ $$\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}=\arcsin u+C,\qquad \int\frac{du}{a^2+u^2}=\frac1a\arctan\frac ua+C,\qquad \int\frac{du}u=\ln|u|+C$$

Worked examples

Example 1 (Fall 2025 Test 3 #8, Review #2): $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}\sec^2x\sqrt{1-\tan^2x}\,dx$
  1. $u=\tan x$, $du=\sec^2x\,dx$.
  2. Limits: $x=0\Rightarrow u=0$, $x=\frac\pi4\Rightarrow u=1$.
  3. $\displaystyle\int_0^1\sqrt{1-u^2}\,du$: the graph of $y=\sqrt{1-u^2}$ is the upper half of the unit circle, so this is a quarter of a disk of radius 1.
  4. Answer: $\dfrac\pi4$.

Marking scheme in the official solution: substitution and limits 3, new integral 3, geometric interpretation 3, value 3.

Example 2 (Fall 2022 Exam 3): $\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{x}{(x^2+1)^4}\,dx$
  1. $u=x^2+1$, $du=2x\,dx\Rightarrow x\,dx=\tfrac12du$; $u:1\to2$.
  2. $\dfrac12\displaystyle\int_1^2u^{-4}\,du=\dfrac12\left[\dfrac{u^{-3}}{-3}\right]_1^2=-\dfrac16\left(\dfrac18-1\right)=\dfrac7{48}$.
Example 3 (Castro, Fall 2018 Final): $\displaystyle\int_{\ln2}^{\ln(2/\sqrt3)}\frac{e^{-x}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2x}}}\,dx$
  1. $u=e^{-x}$, $du=-e^{-x}dx$. Limits: $u(\ln2)=\tfrac12$, $u(\ln\tfrac2{\sqrt3})=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$.
  2. $\displaystyle-\int_{1/2}^{\sqrt3/2}\frac{du}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}=-\left(\arcsin\tfrac{\sqrt3}2-\arcsin\tfrac12\right)=-\left(\tfrac\pi3-\tfrac\pi6\right)=-\dfrac\pi6.$
Example 4 (Fall 2024 MT3): $\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{e^x}{(3-e^x)^2}\,dx$
  1. $u=3-e^x$, $du=-e^xdx$; $x=0\to u=2$, $x=1\to u=3-e$.
  2. $\displaystyle-\int_2^{3-e}u^{-2}du=\left[\frac1u\right]_2^{3-e}=\frac1{3-e}-\frac12=\frac{e-1}{2(3-e)}.$

Common mistakes
  • Changing $dx$ to $du$ but forgetting to change the limits, or changing them and then also substituting back to $x$ (that counts the limits twice).
  • Losing the constant: with $du=2x\,dx$ the factor $\tfrac12$ must stay outside.
  • Getting the minus sign wrong when $du=-\dots\,dx$. Flipping the limits and dropping the minus is fine, but do it on purpose.
  • Integrating $\sqrt{1-u^2}$ term by term. It is a circle; use geometry (or $u=\sin\theta$).
Exam tips
  • Write the three lines every time: “$u=\dots$, $du=\dots$; $x=a\Rightarrow u=\dots$, $x=b\Rightarrow u=\dots$”. These lines alone earn about a third of the marks.
  • If you see $\sqrt{1-(\text{something})^2}$ times the derivative of that something, the answer contains $\pi$.
  • Quick check: if the integrand is positive and $a<b$, the answer must be positive.
